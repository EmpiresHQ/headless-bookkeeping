## Date selection and EU scope

Use the consolidation effective at the tax point, not the original 2006 wording. EUR-Lex lists consolidations dated [2024-01-01](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A02006L0112-20240101), [2025-01-01](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02006L0112-20250101), 2025-03-20 and [2025-04-14 (current listed version)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?qid=1774293721517&uri=CELEX%3A02006L0112-20250414). Some direct text requests returned EUR-Lex robot challenges; indexed primary text and the Commission explanation were available. This is recorded rather than claiming a downloaded complete consolidation. Official Journal amending acts remain authoritative; consolidations are documentation tools.

The following shared classification boundaries are confirmed by the [European Commission place-of-taxation guidance](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/taxation/vat/vat-directive/place-taxation_en):

| Rule | Place | Mandatory evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Art 44, general B2B | Customer establishment; receiving fixed establishment takes precedence | Recipient acting as taxable person; receiving establishment |
| Art 45, general B2C | Supplier establishment supplying the service | Customer status and supplying establishment |
| Art 47, immovable property/accommodation | Property location | Property country |
| Art 48, passenger carriage | Distance in each jurisdiction | Ticket journeys and, where needed, distance allocation |
| Art 53, physical B2B admission | Event location | Admission nature, physical attendance, event country |
| Art 55, restaurant/catering | Actual performance | Performance country; onboard distinction |
| Art 56, short hire | Customer handover location | Continuous duration; vehicle/vessel; handover country |
| Art 57, onboard intra-EU catering | Passenger operation departure | Qualifying intra-EU operation and departure |

Short hire means at most 30 days, or 90 for vessels. Goods, intra-Community acquisition and imports require separate movement/customs analysis. Digital B2C, long hire, margin schemes and use/enjoyment exceptions need specific review rather than a generic B2B default. The shared classifier cannot declare a national exemption or choose a national return row.

### Liability is a later decision

[Directive, 2025-04-14, Arts 44/45 and 192a–196](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?qid=1774293721517&uri=CELEX%3A02006L0112-20250414): Art 196 concerns Art 44 services supplied by a taxable supplier not established in the Member State where VAT is due, to a taxable recipient or VAT-identified non-taxable legal person. Supplier foreign address or a VAT prefix alone does not establish this. Art 192a addresses a local fixed establishment which does not intervene. Art 194 permits Member States to set recipient liability for other non-established supplies: national implementation must be checked rather than extending Art 196 to every special service. Foreign place of supply is not itself proof foreign VAT was charged.

### Virtual admission changes on 2025-01-01

[Directive (EU) 2022/542, Art 1(1)–(2) and Art 3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32022L0542) explicitly excludes virtual attendance from Art 53 and adds customer-location treatment for streamed/virtual B2C activities in Art 54. The relevant measures apply from **2025-01-01**. Accordingly post-boundary B2B virtual admission enters Art 44, after status/establishment validation; B2C virtual activity is not ordinary Art 45. The 2024 consolidation lacks this explicit exclusion. Pre-2025 virtual admission requires dated review; do not retroactively apply the 2025 amendment or assume a physical event country from a platform invoice.

## Estonian adapter

### Passenger treatment

[EMTA passenger guidance, updated 2025-07-03](https://www.emta.ee/en/business-client/taxes-and-payment/value-added-tax/taxation-services/carriage-passengers) explains KMS §15(4)(13): Estonia's part of international passenger carriage is zero-rated where the ticket's departure and destination are in different countries; personal luggage and personal means of transport are included. A domestic service independently supplied to an intermediary can remain standard-rated even when the intermediary sells an international through-ticket. Technical interruptions do not alone change the international journey. Fully foreign carriage has no Estonian place of supply; preserve this distinction from an Estonian zero-rated portion, even though the guidance describes its Estonian zero-rate treatment under §15(4)(1).

A return itinerary must retain constituent journeys: Tallinn–Copenhagen and Copenhagen–Tallinn are cross-border legs, although the whole return itinerary starts and ends in Estonia. Ancillary software, unrelated administration or separately supplied services need their own evidence and classification. Airline identity is not tax evidence. A zero-rated purchased ticket creates neither ordinary reverse-charge VAT nor a deductible input amount or foreign-acquisition reporting base; it is not the purchaser's zero-rated sales turnover.

### Rates and tax point

[EMTA standard-rate guidance](https://www.emta.ee/en/node/336/chapter/83209/pdf), updated 2025-07-29, confirms 24% from **2025-07-01**, and explains historical 20% transitional exceptions which ended 2025-06-30. [EMTA VAT changes](https://www.emta.ee/en/business-client/taxes-and-payment/value-added-tax) and the [Estonian Act consolidation effective 2025-01-01](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/111122024009) support the prior 22% rate; the standard schedule is 20% through 2023, 22% from 2024-01-01 through 2025-06-30, 24% thereafter. Invoice date alone need not create supply: advances and continuous supply can affect the tax point. Unsupported transitional arrangements must hold for evidence. Reversals preserve original measurement; corrected replacements use the legally correct tax point and facts. This schedule does not assign the standard rate to accommodation or other reduced-rate services.

### Reporting origin and deduction

The [EMTA form index](https://www.emta.ee/en/business-client/taxes-and-payment/tax-returns-exchange-information/vat-return-forms-vd-and-vdp), updated 2026-04-09, identifies separate KMD forms for 2024, 2025-01-01–2025-06-30 and from 2025-07-01. Use the form appropriate to the period; English forms are informational translations.

The [KMD form from 2025-07-01, instructions pp. 4–5](https://www.emta.ee/sites/default/files/documents/2025-07/vorm_kmd_2025_07_eng.pdf) puts qualifying intra-Community goods acquisitions and services from another Member State's taxable person in row 6 (goods also 6.1). Row 7 covers specified other recipient-taxed acquisitions, including foreign business suppliers not registered in Estonia, excluding row 6. Imports use their separate path, including import-tax rows where legally authorized. Row 5 is only legally deductible input VAT; partial entitlement reduces it independently of output liability. Row 3 is zero-rated supplies, not every purchase with a zero tax amount. Historical 20%/22% corrections retain their rate-specific rows on the newer form.

[EMTA deduction conditions](https://www.emta.ee/en/admin/content/handbook_article/150), updated 2025-01-08, requires buyer/document evidence; another person's invoice cannot establish our input entitlement. Simplified passenger invoices have limited exceptions and a EUR160 exclusive-of-tax ceiling. For reverse charge, other evidence such as contracts may replace the invoice (§31(2)–(3)); imports require customs evidence. Registration, business use and deduction entitlement are independent facts. Foreign VAT cannot become Estonian deductible input VAT merely through classification. Unknown ownership is not resolved by uploading a document.
